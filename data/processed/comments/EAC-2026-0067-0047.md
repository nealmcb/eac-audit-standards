**Date:** 2026-05-20

I write in strong support of the EAC’s proposed improvements to the Voluntary National Standards for Election Audits and urge the Commission to adopt the four key reforms highlighted by the Michigan Fair Elections Institute (MFEI) in their April 26, 2025 letter (see attached), along with the specific edits proposed by Michael Raisch, CPA. As a Certified Public Accountant (CPA) with an MBA and over 25 years of experience as a Supervisory Auditor in the Department of Defense, I have conducted and overseen numerous audits performed in full conformance with Generally Accepted Government Auditing Standards (GAGAS / Yellow Book). Independence is not optional under GAGAS—it is foundational. When election officials or entities within the election system audit themselves, the process fails the most basic test of objectivity and public credibility. I fully endorse: - Creation of an Independent Audit Committee to administer audits, removing them from any entity with a stake in the outcome. - Enhanced County-Level Reporting that goes beyond mere verification of results and requires granular performance data on every jurisdiction. - Full Access to All Election Artifacts for auditors, with appropriate safeguards to protect voter confidentiality. Audits without access to the underlying records are audits in name only. - Mandatory Substantive Financial and Compliance Audits of HAVA Grant Recipients under GAGAS standards. States that fail to comply with HAVA requirements (including voter roll maintenance, accurate voter histories, and proper use of federal funds) should face immediate suspension, claw-back of funds, and accountability for responsible individuals, exactly as MFEI recommends. In addition to these reforms, I recommend the standards explicitly require statistical sampling (e.g., random selection of precincts, ballots, voter records, and poll books) as a core audit procedure. Statistical methods provide defensible, representative evidence of systemic issues such as voter roll accuracy, duplicate or outdated registrations, and anomalies in voting history—issues that self-audits by election administrators have repeatedly failed to detect. GAGAS explicitly supports the use of statistical sampling when it is the most efficient and effective way to obtain sufficient, appropriate audit evidence. The current practice of allowing election officials to audit their own work creates the exact appearance of bias that GAGAS was designed to prevent. The EAC has a historic opportunity to set a national model that restores public confidence by insisting on true independence, complete access, rigorous sampling, and real financial accountability for HAVA funds. I respectfully urge the Commission to incorporate these changes before finalizing the standards. The Michigan Fair Elections Institute’s attached one-page summary and supporting materials provide concrete examples of why these reforms are urgently needed. Respectfully submitted, Mark Vaeth CPA/MBA Livonia, MI

Dear Commissioners Palmer and Hovland:

On behalf of the Michigan Fair Elections Institute (MFEI), a 501(c)(3) nonprofit organization dedicated to election integrity research and public education, I write to express strong support for the proposed changes to the EAC’s audit standards currently under consideration. We urge the Commission to adopt all four of the following reforms.

**1. Independent Audit Committee.** Administration of election audits should be shifted from entities within the election system to an independent audit committee. Audits conducted by parties with a stake in the outcome cannot command public confidence. Independence is the foundation of credibility.

**2. Enhanced County-Level Reporting.** Audit standards should require additional reporting on county performance, beyond mere verification of results. Granular, jurisdiction-level data is essential for identifying systemic weaknesses and ensuring accountability at every level of election administration.

**3. Full Access to Election Artifacts.** Auditors must have access to all election artifacts, with appropriate protections to maintain voter confidentiality. Audits that cannot examine the underlying record are audits in name only

**4.** **States that receive HAVA grant funding** should be subject to substantive, financial audits in compliance with Yellow Book, Generally Accepted Government Accounting Standards (GAGAS). Violators should have their funds clawed back and individuals should be held accountable. See “Part B: Inadequate Scope of Federal Audits Systemic Deficiencies in Government Agency Audits of HAVA grants and an Urgent Request for the Government Accountability Office to Investigate,” *MFEI Investigation of Michigan Elections,* Sept. 2025, pages 64-70.<https://b6d83fed-d9cb-4cb1-9e2f-60cfa1575f8d.filesusr.com/ugd/aa3979_edb360768ac042f0aa5245902b401b52.pdf>

MFEI has conducted extensive comparative research on election administration structures across the states. We have seen firsthand how the absence of independent oversight and complete audit access undermines public trust and leaves systemic vulnerabilities undetected. These four reforms would constitute a meaningful and durable improvement to the national audit standard. An excerpt from MFEI’s report illustrates the fundamental issues currently hampering the EAC’s effectiveness:

*Two core questions plague the audit of Michigan’s usage of federal HAVA funds.*

*1. Did federal auditors properly considerstakeholder input about Michigan's election integrity concerns when conducting the $49.88 million HAVA grant audit in 2024? 2. What did the federal auditors do to test whether Michigan's certification was in compliance with all HAVA requirements as required by GAGAS 8.90 and 8.98? It appears the federal auditors focused narrowly on financial compliance under 2 CFR 200 while failing to evaluate critical HAVA mandates, such as voter access, election technology security, voter education, and adequate voter roll maintenance, as required by Titles I and II (52 U.S.C. §§ 20901, 21081).*

We thank the Commission for its consideration and encourage prompt adoption of these changes. A strong final standard will provide model language that state legislatures across the country can act upon. Please do not hesitate to contact us if MFEI can provide additional research or testimony in support of this effort.

## **Issues:** 

- Many of Michigan’s election practices and procedures are not compliant with federal election laws. 

- The number of votes continues to outnumber the number of recorded voters over the past two elections, and the QVF output has puzzling changes in voting history. 

- Michigan’s Qualified Voter File (QVF) has more registrations (103.1%) than the state’s voting age population. (See chart.) 

- Membership in the Electronic Registration Information Center (ERIC) and noncompliant guidance from the state’s top election official, the Secretary of State are largely responsible for Michigan’s elections issues. 

- The QVF has multiple voter identifiers (IDs) in direct contravention to HAVA requirements. 

- The result is a potential form of Voter Suppression in which cast false ballots could cancel the votes of legitimate voters. 

- Recent events and revelations indicate the potential for fraud is real and present. 

- Recent changes to Michigan’s Constitution and state elections laws are contrary to HAVA, the Driver’s Privacy Protection Act, the National Voting Rights Act, and other federal voting rights laws.
