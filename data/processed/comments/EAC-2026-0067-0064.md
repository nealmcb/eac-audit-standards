# EAC-2026-0067-0064 — Anonymous (Enhanced Voting)

**Date:** 2026-05-20
**Docket:** EAC-2026-0067
**Source:** [Regulations.gov](https://www.regulations.gov/comment/EAC-2026-0067-0064)

---

Both international and national efforts for auditing in the public sector already exist and are well-tested, raising the question of whether the EAC developing audit standards is necessary. The International Organization of Supreme Audit Institutions exists specifically to (among other things) set standards for public sector auditing, and the Government Accountability Office acts as the supreme audit institution for the United States. The GAO is already trusted to provide standards for internal controls on government auditing within federal and state institutions. This makes the EAC defining these both duplicative and, given the trust deficit in U.S. elections, risky. To successfully define a specific set of auditing standards outside those that are already trusted in other sectors requires a building of trust that would be unnecessary if anchored to existing, respected, and trusted audit standards. To that end, we recommend the EAC focus on defining the specific needs for the various audits in U.S. elections and not attempt to define broad standards for auditing.
