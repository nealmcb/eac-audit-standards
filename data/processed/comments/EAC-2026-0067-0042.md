1) Objective Standards: a. Authorization: Is it necessary that an audit be conducted at the direction of the chief election official at the state or local level? What if that official is elected and on the ballot for that election? If the entity is perceived to lack sufficient credibility or capacity, having a senior official who is more insulated from the political process overseeing the audit could be helpful. Additional technical support may be necessary in some cases as well. b. Funding: Ideally, audits are adequately funded by applicable public resources, but what if there isn’t sufficient public funding to carry them out successfully. Private funding has previously helped successfully administer elections, and there may be a case for allowing private funding if public funding doesn’t come through and there are sufficient safeguards for ensuring that the private financing doesn’t compromise the independent and accountability of the election processes. c. Impartiality: I might clarify and expound a bit more on this standard. For example, I think I know what you mean when you say that audits must be conducted without bias or prejudice. But everyone has a bit of bias and prejudice, no matter how much they try and keep it in check. I think it’s better to acknowledge this like you further down in the word document, and instead say something like, “Audits must be driven by factual evidence to reach fair and objective conclusions. Relying on things like assumptions, suppositions, or and hearsay is not sufficient.”
