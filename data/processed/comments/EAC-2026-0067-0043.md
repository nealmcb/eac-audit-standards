# EAC-2026-0067-0043 — Doug Befort

**Date:** 2026-05-20
**Docket:** EAC-2026-0067

---

While election officials are required to comply with election laws, they have considerable discretion over how they manage election operations and how they document the election processes. The internal operations and operating procedures are not transparent despite public records laws. As such, many election officials operate without enough transparency for the public to understand, question and thoughtfully scrutinize election operations. For decades the operations of public and government entities have been routinely audited by independent outside parties to determine if they do a good job. I am not aware of any election office that is subject to periodic independent audits. As such, the public does not really know if our election offices do a good job. Fair and transparent elections are the fundamental principal on which our Republic was founded. As such, the public deserves to know if election operations are sound and produce reliable results. Making election offices subject to independent audits is necessary and will shine a light on those election officers that do not do a good job. The EAC’s voluntary standards do not provide for independent audits. When election officials plan, execute and report the results of an audit, it is not an independent audit. Every significant election process should be subject to periodic audits by outside independent parties. Examples of Some Key Election Processes:  Design of effective internal controls and processes  Adherence to effective internal controls  Documentation of policies and procedures  Processing of voter registration applications  Completeness and accuracy of list maintenance activity  Design and testing of cyber security posture  Processing and tracking of vote-by-mail envelopes and ballots  Aggregation of election results  Reporting of election results  Processing and response to public records requests  Storage and organization of paper ballots
