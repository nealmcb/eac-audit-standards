Detailed entries

The entries below give a fuller summary of each commenter’s position, in numerical comment-ID order. For the 16 submissions with file attachments, the most reliable read of the position comes from opening the PDF/DOCX in data/raw/attachments/{comment-id}/; where the CSV comment_text contains substantive material, that is summarized here.

0002 — Ronald Rivest

Submitted from the Boston area. Reads as a brief peer-review by a foundational figure in the risk-limiting audit (RLA) literature. Rivest praises the draft as “excellent” but identifies four omissions: (1) no discussion of statistical audits such as RLAs or how to explain them to non-technical audiences; (2) no treatment of the fact that an RLA’s workload varies with the margin of victory; (3) no guidance on which contests to audit when resources are limited; (4) no guidance on choosing ballots at random. The comment is constructive and additive rather than critical.

0003 — Stephanie Singer

Submitted from El Cerrito, CA. A single concrete drafting suggestion: add a new row to the “Auditing Activities Before, During, and After an Election” table for an Anomaly Audit, defined as a statistical analysis of election data to identify and investigate significant anomalies.

0004 — Michelle Burchill

Submitted from Arizona (ZIP 86314). Speaks as someone who has worked in elections “for several years and cycles.” Largely positive about the system but feels jurisdictions only do comprehensive audits “when something went wrong,” and supports outside organizations providing expert evaluation. Explicitly likes the option of a separate tabulation audit that examines all ballots, not just a sample.

0005 — Civera (organization)

Vendor of an “ElectionShield” product. Argues that statistical/forensic analysis of election results — the kind of anomaly detection their product performs — should become a standard security practice for all U.S. elections. Cites the federal conviction of former Congressman Ozzie Myers for ballot-box stuffing in Philadelphia as a real-world case where such analysis was useful. The comment functions partly as a product pitch.

0006 — Gregory Lawson

Submitted from Camano Island, WA. Tells the EAC the draft is too long and gets into the weeds. Recommends each state/county form an Audit Committee — current and former election officials plus at-large members — that would design the audit details and hire an independent out-of-state auditor for each cycle. Asks for rotating membership and floats a credential / training program as a new profession.

0008 — Julie Hall

Submitted from Attleboro, MA. Short and pointed: the audit needs to be done by an independent third party or the State Auditor, who has both staff and mission. Letting the Secretary of State perform the audit is “putting the hen in charge of the hen house.”

0010 — dave cantera

Submitted from NJ. One sentence: “audit for any industry is based on independent observation by disinterested party or organizations.” Implicit rejection of internal audits.

0011 — Denise Flohr

Brief. Insists audits must be done “independently and forensically.” Auditing yourself, she writes, is unrealistic given the state of public morals; independent forensic audits are needed so voters can be confident their votes count.

0012 — Ohio Election Integrity Network (organization)

0013 — Stephen Aleshire

0038 — Richard Koury

Submitted from Grants Pass, OR. The most structured individual technical submission. Seven points: 1. Audits run by an independent Audit Committee, not by those who run elections or maintain voter rolls. 2. Auditors must have access to all records from paper ballots through the Cast Vote Record with full chain-of-custody data. 3. The audit must reconcile the ballot count, voter count, and CVR, plus any state-mandated tests. 4. Defects must be documented and secured for future planning. 5. Audits should use valid statistical methods set by the Audit Committee. 6. Auditors must be trained in election audit best practices. 7. The Audit Committee must report back to each county.

0040 — Anonymous

0041 — Robert Kufert

Submitted from Panama City Beach, FL. Calls the draft an “excellent general standards document” but says it doesn’t address where fraud risk is highest. Specifically asks for audit attention to: Vote-by-Mail (envelope/ballot production, intake, storage, reconciliation, voter-ID), UOCAVA ballots (domestic address verification), preventing non-citizen registration across all four registration paths, and voter-roll cleanup (frequency, methods, removal of non-citizens and inactive voters).

0042 — Anonymous

A drafting-level pass on three of the listed standards. Authorization: flags the awkwardness of having an audit run by the same chief election official who may be on the ballot; suggests a more politically insulated senior official. Funding: ideal is public funding, but notes private funding has been used successfully and could be allowed with safeguards. Impartiality: acknowledge that everyone has some bias rather than promising bias-free conduct; suggests rewording to require fact- and evidence-driven judgment rather than assumption or hearsay.

0043 — Doug Befort

Argues that the draft’s “voluntary standards do not provide for independent audits.” Lists 11 election processes that should be subject to periodic outside-party audits: internal controls design, adherence to controls, policy and procedure documentation, processing of voter-registration applications, completeness and accuracy of list maintenance, cyber-security design and testing, VBM envelope/ballot tracking, results aggregation, results reporting, FOIA processing, and paper-ballot storage. Frames election offices as government entities that should be audited like any other.

0044 — Michigan Fair Elections Institute (Patrice Johnson, Chair and Founder)

Substantive in-CSV letter to Commissioners Palmer and Hovland, addressing the Commission, plus a PDF attachment. Identifies the four reforms that become the most-cited reform package across the docket: 1. Independent Audit Committee — administration shifted out of any entity within the election system. 2. Enhanced county-level reporting — granular, jurisdiction-level performance data, not just verification of results. 3. Full access to election artifacts — auditors must see the underlying record. 4. Mandatory financial / compliance audits of HAVA recipients under GAGAS (“Yellow Book”), with funding clawbacks for violators. The letter cites MFEI’s September 2025 report criticizing how federal auditors scoped the $49.88M Michigan HAVA grant audit (focused on 2 CFR 200 compliance rather than testing HAVA mandates on voter access, technology security, education, and voter-roll maintenance). Contact: P.O. Box 41, Stockbridge, MI 49285, 517-420-7978, patrice@mifairelections.org.

0067 — Barry Chapman

Submitted from VA (ZIP 22309). The heaviest individual technical/framework submission. Chapman has audited under ISO-9001, ISO 10000, ISO 27001, CMMI-DEV, CMMI-SVC, federal agencies, and NIST SP800-53a control sets; he has also developed risk frameworks and work breakdown structures for U.S. election systems. Identifies the U.S. election system as 646 distinct processes across 57 areas (NIST Election Model of 2019). Recommends: 1. List standards by type of audit (process, equipment, data, compliance) and by election-process sequence (before, during, after), traceable to established frameworks. 2. Move audit ownership out of the SoS or Elections Office — typically to the Inspector General under the Governor — with shared authority across political-party observers. 3. Credentialed auditors trained in election audits. 4. Defects logged as incidents, classified major (count-affecting) or minor (process-affecting), root-caused and remediated. 5. Reference the NIST 646-process WBS for consistency (offers to provide it).

0070 — Victoria Cruz (second submission)

Submitted from Athens, GA. Cruz’s second submission to the docket, after 0049. Argues voter-roll integrity is a precondition for any meaningful election audit, citing researcher Andrew Paquette’s published work on “intelligence-grade” algorithmic anomalies in voter rolls in NY, AZ, GA, WI, TX, OH and others, and a recent Bexar County, TX primary in which a campaign’s early-voting list reportedly contained hundreds of inauthentic registration entries. Concludes that voter rolls may be effectively unauditable and proposes nationwide in-person re-registration with documentary citizenship proof; asks the EAC to set voter-roll security standards.

0071 — Richard Klabechek

Submitted from MN (55303). An essay titled “Radical Legibility.” Argues a constitutional republic should demand verification, not faith in machines; calls for replacing proprietary tabulation “black boxes” with manual precinct-level hand counts performed in public by neighbors. Rejects the “tabulation industry” in favor of a public “tabulation ceremony.”

0072 — Anonymous

Concerned the word “Voluntary” in the title will give states permission to ignore the standards. Suggests alternative naming such as “Best Practices” or “Standards for Achieving Secure Elections.” Also asks for manual hand-count verification and audits of ballots by valid voters to be included.

Themes across the record

A few patterns stand out across the 71 comments and are worth keeping in mind when reading individual entries:

  • The dominant theme — overwhelmingly — is independence. Roughly two-thirds of comments insist audits be done by a third party, with most rejecting any role for the Secretary of State, election department, or election vendors. The Michigan Fair Elections Institute’s framing (Independent Audit Committee + county-level reporting + full artifact access + GAGAS audits of HAVA recipients) is referenced and endorsed by many individual commenters.
  • A small but technically heavy minority wants the EAC to lean harder on statistical methods — Rivest, McBurnett, Verified Voting on RLAs; Vaeth, Civera, Singer on sampling and anomaly detection.
  • Several commenters (Enhanced Voting, Chapman) want the EAC to defer to existing audit frameworks (GAO/GAGAS, INTOSAI, NIST/ISO/CMMI) rather than create election-specific general standards.
  • A vocal subset (Klabechek, several Texas commenters, the 0072 commenter, parts of Cruz’s submissions) is skeptical of machine tabulation altogether and wants hand counting, sometimes coupled with voter-roll integrity demands.
  • Most short individual comments are essentially “third-party audits only,” with little drafting-level engagement; the most detailed engagement comes from the eight or so commenters with accounting/audit backgrounds (Raisch, Vaeth, Schaeper, Kufert, Chapman, Koury, Befort, Lawson) and the organizational submissions.
